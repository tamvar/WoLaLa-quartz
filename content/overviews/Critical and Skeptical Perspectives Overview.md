## Summary

This strand tracks arguments that push back against inflated claims about language models. It covers skepticism about meaning, understanding, reference, cognition, linguistic generalization, and explanatory overreach, while keeping those criticisms linked to the neighboring positive or reconstructive strands.

## Scope

This overview collects critiques that matter for WoLaLa because they sharpen standards for what should count as linguistic competence, semantic success, understanding, or explanatory adequacy.

It overlaps heavily with strands 3, 5, 7, and 9, but the overlap should be handled by cross-links rather than duplicate synthesis.

## Central Questions

- Which strong claims about language models are criticized most directly in the current wiki?
- What distinctions do skeptical sources use to separate fluent output from understanding?
- When critics reject semantic or cognitive claims, what standards are they actually invoking?
- Which skeptical arguments target current evaluation practice rather than language models as such?

## Principal Subtopics

- form-only versus meaning-rich accounts of language understanding
- skepticism about reference, semantics, and linguistic cognition claims
- critiques of reading generation quality as evidence of understanding
- historical arguments about intentionality, computation, and strong AI
- critical replies to ambitious claims about human-like linguistic generalization

## Major Positions or Debates

- whether next-token prediction can support meaning or understanding claims
- whether reference can be derivative or historically inherited rather than fully self-standing
- whether LLM competence is linguistic, cognitive, merely behavioral, or only superficial
- whether contemporary models are good theories of human linguistic cognition

- [[../sources/Source - Common Sense, the Turing Test, and the Quest for Real AI|Common Sense, the Turing Test, and the Quest for Real AI]] — `existing_deep`; critical; central; high. Strong common-sense critique of treating fluent dialogue as evidence of genuine intelligence.

- [[../sources/Source - Why Large Language Models Are Poor Theories of Human Linguistic Cognition|Why Large Language Models Are Poor Theories of Human Linguistic Cognition]] — `existing_brief`; critical; central; high. Direct reply source for theory-of-language and cognition claims.
- [[../sources/Source - Why Linguistics Will Thrive in the 21st Century|Why Linguistics Will Thrive in the 21st Century]] — `existing_brief`; critical; central; medium. Direct reply insisting that predictive success does not replace interpretable linguistic theory or solve the acquisition problem.
- [[../sources/Source - Chomsky's Galilean Explanatory Style|Chomsky's Galilean Explanatory Style]] — `existing_deep`; methodological; supporting; high. Defense of idealized competence explanation that also clarifies where auxiliary assumptions and theory-guided evidence can insulate a program.
- [[../sources/Source - Testing AI on Language Comprehension Tasks Reveals Insensitivity to Underlying Meaning|Testing AI on Language Comprehension Tasks Reveals Insensitivity to Underlying Meaning]] — `existing_brief`; critical; central; high. - [[../sources/Source - Noam Chomsky The False Promise of ChatGPT|Noam Chomsky: The False Promise of ChatGPT]] — `existing_brief`; historical; supporting; medium. High-visibility public statement of the anti-LLM explanatory critique.

Reasoning-focused source arguing that semantic scaffolding, not robust symbolic reasoning, explains much in-context success.
- [[../sources/Source - Large Language Models Pass the Turing Test|Large Language Models Pass the Turing Test]] — `existing_brief`; recent; supporting; medium. Behavioral substitutability result useful for separating imitation success from deeper understanding claims.
