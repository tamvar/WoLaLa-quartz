## Summary

This strand tracks historically important background for current WoLaLa disputes. It covers earlier debates about language, mind, artificial intelligence, reference, intentionality, computation, and explanatory ambition that still shape how present-day language models are interpreted. Its role is not to catalogue old sources, but to map the lineages that illuminate current LLM arguments.

## Scope

The strand includes classic and revival sources that bear on current disputes about understanding, meaning, cognition, and model-building. Many of these appear in the repository as structured containers or background volumes rather than as single-purpose LLM papers.

## Central Questions

- Which older debates best illuminate present arguments about LLM meaning and understanding?
- How do earlier visions of mind design, computation, and language compare with current modeling claims?
- Which historical sources are already deeply represented, and which remain only indexed background?

## Principal Subtopics

- intentionality, understanding, and strong AI
- philosophy-of-language background for meaning and reference
- historical mind-design and artificial-intelligence visions
- Wittgensteinian and distributional prehistories of language modeling

## Major Positions or Debates

- program execution versus understanding
- semantic content versus formal computation
- language as use, reference, or inferential role
- ambitious explanatory programs versus more modest performance-centered claims
