## Summary
WoLaLa Wiki is a source-grounded research wiki about language models and language. This public subset is meant to be read as a curated introduction to a set of questions about meaning, linguistic structure, mechanistic explanation, and the interpretation of language-model behavior.

## What You Will Find Here

The current public subset is organized around three connected strands. One asks what language-model success does or does not show about meaning, grounding, and reference. Another looks at what linguistic structure can be extracted from neural representations. A third focuses on mechanistic evidence about induction heads and in-context learning.

These strands are meant to be explored as topic bundles rather than as isolated pages. Each bundle has an overview that orients the reader, a small set of concept pages that stabilize recurring ideas, and analysis pages that work through the main interpretive questions.

## Main Topic Bundles

### Meaning, Reference, and Distributional Language
This bundle is the best place to begin if you are interested in what language models may or may not mean, refer to, or understand.

Begin with [[overviews/Meaning, Reference, and Distributional Language Overview|Meaning, Reference, and Distributional Language Overview]], then follow it into [[concepts/Meaning and Reference in Language Models|Meaning and Reference in Language Models]] or [[analyses/What Would Count as Meaning or Reference in a Language Model|What Would Count as Meaning or Reference in a Language Model]].

### Neural NLP Probing
This bundle focuses on what kinds of linguistic structure are readable from model representations, and on how cautiously such evidence should be interpreted.

Begin with [[overviews/Neural NLP Probing Overview|Neural NLP Probing Overview]], then continue to [[concepts/Probing Classifiers|Probing Classifiers]] or [[analyses/What Probing Evidence Can Support|What Probing Evidence Can Support]].

### Induction Heads and In-Context Learning
This bundle focuses on circuit-level and intervention-based evidence about in-context learning, especially the role of induction heads.

Begin with [[overviews/Induction Heads and In-Context Learning Overview|Induction Heads and In-Context Learning Overview]], then continue to [[concepts/Induction Heads|Induction Heads]] or [[analyses/Evidence That Induction Heads Support In-Context Learning|Evidence That Induction Heads Support In-Context Learning]].
