## Summary
Chen, Gao, and He study whether prompting large language models can improve factual-consistency evaluation for summaries. They find that LLM-based evaluators can outperform earlier factuality systems across several settings, making the paper a useful bridge between language-focused evaluation and practical scholarly assessment workflows.

It is useful because it shows a concrete evaluation use-case where LLMs function not as the object being judged but as evaluators. That matters for WoLaLa's method strand: the paper compares prompting styles, model families, and summarization outputs in a way that makes evaluation design itself the central issue.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The source is about factuality evaluation for summaries, not a general solution to language-model truthfulness. The open question is how far this evaluator role can be trusted outside the relatively structured summarization setting.
