## Summary
Warstadt and Bowman argue that artificial neural networks can be genuinely useful for language-acquisition debates, but only if the comparison with human learners is disciplined. Their central point is that today's most successful language models have major unfair advantages over children, especially in data scale, so successful learnability simulations are informative only when the model learner is made more plausibly human-like. For WoLaLa, the chapter is a concise methodological bridge between acquisition theory, neural modeling, and evidence standards.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is valuable because it rejects two easy positions at once. It rejects the idea that current neural learners straightforwardly settle human acquisition debates, because their input conditions and biases differ radically from those of children. But it also rejects the idea that model-based acquisition work is irrelevant. Properly constrained artificial learners can test whether a target linguistic generalization is learnable under a proposed human learning scenario and can put pressure on poverty-of-the-stimulus arguments.

## Key Points
- Learnability evidence matters only when model learners are not given overwhelming advantages over human children.
- Current language models are trained on vastly more linguistic input than children receive.
- Better acquisition modeling may require multimodal input and interaction, not only text.
- Neural learners can function as experimental tools for causal manipulation of learning conditions.
- The paper is strongest as a methodological framework, not as a demonstration that current systems already model child language acquisition well.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The chapter is programmatic and methodological rather than a single empirical demonstration. Its value lies in setting evidential standards for future work. The main open question is how far suitably constrained neural learners can go before one needs stronger domain-specific prior structure to explain human acquisition.
