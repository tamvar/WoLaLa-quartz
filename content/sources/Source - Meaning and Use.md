## Summary
*Meaning and Use: Papers Presented at the Second Jerusalem Philosophical Encounter, April 1976*, edited by Avishai Margalit, is a historically important source container on one of the core background questions for WoLaLa: should language be approached primarily through truth-conditional meaning and reference, or through communicative use, pragmatics, and linguistic practice? This note treats the volume selectively rather than as a single doctrinal intervention. Its main WoLaLa value is that it brings Quine, Davidson, Dummett, Searle, Putnam, Kripke, Hintikka, and others into one explicitly staged confrontation over meaning, use, theory of language, and related philosophical method.

## Selective Scope
This source page treats the collection selectively as a bridge for:

- the meaning-versus-use framing in philosophy of language
- truth-conditional, referential, and inferential approaches to meaning
- pragmatics, speech acts, and intentionality
- historical background for present LLM debates about semantics and understanding
- the relation between semantic theory and ordinary linguistic practice

## Key Points
- The collection is organized around the question whether meaning should be treated as prior to use, use as prior to meaning, or the two as mutually constraining.
- The table of contents makes the volume especially useful for WoLaLa because it gathers several figures whose positions still structure current semantic debates.
- Quine, Davidson, Dummett, Searle, Putnam, Kripke, and Strawson appear here in a way that highlights disagreement rather than canonizing one consensus view.
- The preface explicitly rejects a simple priority claim between meaning and use, framing the two as equally necessary and complementary.
- The volume is therefore a strong historical background source for current disputes about whether linguistic behavior, inferential role, public use, or world-involving reference should ground semantic interpretation.

## Details
The local file identifies the book as a 1979 edited volume drawn from the Second Jerusalem Philosophical Encounter of April 1976 and dedicated to Yehoshua Bar-Hillel. Margalit's preface explains the framing question directly: should natural language be approached through formal semantics centered on truth and reference, or through communicative use, speech acts, and linguistic practice? The preface then refuses a simple winner, arguing instead that meaning and use are equally necessary.

The table of contents shows why the volume matters for WoLaLa. Quine's "Use and Its Place in Meaning" pushes on the relation between use and semantic theory. Davidson's "Moods and Performances" and Searle's "Intentionality and the Use of Language" keep act-theoretic and pragmatic pressures visible. Dummett asks what the appeal to use does for the theory of meaning, while Putnam's "Reference and Understanding" and Kripke's "A Puzzle about Belief" sharpen how reference and semantic grasp come apart from simpler usage pictures. Hintikka and Carlson contribute formal-semantic and game-theoretic material, while Margalit's "Open Texture" and Dascal's "Conversational Relevance" broaden the field further.

That makes the volume more than background canon. It functions as a concentrated map of several approaches that current LLM debates still reactivate in new form. Skeptics of strong LLM semantics often insist that text-distribution alone cannot secure meaning, reference, or understanding. More permissive positions sometimes emphasize inferential role, public use, or derivative reference. This collection shows that the deeper philosophical terrain behind those contemporary arguments was already highly plural. It also shows why present debates so easily slide past one another: different participants may be defending different notions of meaning, understanding, or semantic success.

## Interpretation
This source should be treated as a selective deep source container. It does not speak with one voice, and it should not be reduced to a simple `meaning versus use` slogan. Its value lies in keeping the older semantic landscape visible, especially the fact that truth-conditional, use-theoretic, speech-act, intentional, and referential approaches were already being tested against one another within the same forum.

## Limitation Or Open Question
Because this is a conference volume, the individual chapters vary in topic, style, and argumentative target. The main open question is which contained line of dispute would most reward later focused treatment: Quine on use, Dummett on use-theoretic meaning, Searle on intentionality, Putnam on reference, or Kripke on belief and semantic opacity.
