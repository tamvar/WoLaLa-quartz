## Summary
Michael Tomasello's *Constructing a Language: A Usage-Based Theory of Language Acquisition* is a foundational usage-based alternative to strongly nativist and formalist accounts of language learning. This note treats the book selectively rather than comprehensively. Its main importance for WoLaLa is that it offers a sustained account of how children could build words, constructions, abstract syntax, morphology, and discourse competence from intention-reading, pattern-finding, communicative practice, and cultural learning rather than from a richly pre-specified universal grammar.

## Selective Scope
This page treats the volume selectively as a WoLaLa anchor for:

- usage-based linguistics as an alternative to strong formalism
- construction learning and abstract syntactic generalization
- word learning, morphology, and discourse as parts of one developmental picture
- intention-reading and pattern-finding as core learning capacities
- the relation between language acquisition, cultural transmission, and communicative function

## Key Points
- Tomasello rejects the idea that the most important parts of linguistic competence must be innately specified as formal universal grammar.
- He treats language acquisition as construction learning driven by communicative use, analogy, abstraction, and cultural convention.
- The book links words, constructions, morphology, discourse, and social cognition into one developmental framework rather than treating syntax as an isolated formal core.
- Intention-reading and pattern-finding are presented as the core enabling capacities for language learning.
- The source matters for WoLaLa because it provides one of the clearest strong alternatives to anti-statistical and strongly nativist readings of linguistic competence.

## Details
The table of contents shows the breadth of the book's argument. After setting out usage-based linguistics, Tomasello moves through the origins of language, words, early syntactic constructions, abstract syntactic constructions, nominal and clausal constructions, complex constructions and discourse, and finally broader biological, cultural, and ontogenetic processes. That structure matters because it treats linguistic development as continuous with broader cognition and interaction rather than as the deployment of a separate formal module.

For WoLaLa, the central argumentative pressure comes from the contrast with Chomskyan views. Tomasello repeatedly targets the assumption that abstract grammar must be built in because usage-based learning could not reach the relevant generalizations. Instead, he argues that children learn from richly structured communicative input using domain-general pattern-finding capacities together with human-specific intention-reading and social-cognitive skills. Linguistic abstractions emerge from use, comparison, categorization, and the conventionalization of recurrent constructions.

This makes the book especially useful for strand 4. It is not a direct book about language models, but it offers a well-developed theory-facing alternative for interpreting what counts as a plausible explanation of linguistic generalization. The source does not say that all statistical learning is shallow; rather, it insists that meaningful language learning is inseparable from communicative function, constructional organization, and developmental process. That can support more sympathetic readings of some modern language-model results than strong nativism does, while still resisting the idea that benchmark performance alone settles the theoretical issue.

The book also matters for strand 5 because it embeds language in shared intentionality, cultural learning, and interaction. In that respect it complements the shorter Tomasello works already in the wiki, but it does much more organizing work. It is a foundational long-form statement of a usage-based developmental picture rather than a single bridge claim.

## Interpretation
This source should be read as a selective deep theoretical anchor, not as a complete summary of all 399 pages. Its main role in WoLaLa is to keep the generative-versus-usage-based dispute live in a form strong enough to organize later comparisons. It is one of the few sources in the current repository that can support sustained contrast among constructionist learning, formal linguistic theory, and current model-based evidence.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models Overview|Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives Overview|Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives]]. It matters because debates about whether LLMs challenge Chomskyan skepticism cannot be evaluated well if the only alternatives are generic "statistics" or generic "deep learning." Tomasello supplies a structured usage-based alternative with concrete claims about constructions, abstraction, communication, and learning.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The book is a theory of human acquisition, not a study of current language models. The open question is how much of its usage-based picture can be productively compared with LLM learning, and how much depends on human social development in ways that text-trained models do not share.
