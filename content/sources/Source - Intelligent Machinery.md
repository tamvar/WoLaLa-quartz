## Summary
Alan Turing’s *Intelligent Machinery* is an early historical anchor for thinking about machine intelligence as a design and explanatory problem rather than only a matter of imitation or practical automation. For WoLaLa, the source matters because it helps place contemporary debates about model capability, architecture, and intelligence claims within a much older tradition of asking what sort of machinery could count as intelligent and why.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports strand 9 and secondarily strand 7. It gives the historical strand a genuine early backbone rather than a retrospective commentary alone, and it helps the mind-design strand show that reverse-engineering ambition did not begin with mechanistic interpretability. The page is especially useful as a compact reference when current discussions drift too quickly from present benchmarks to apparently unprecedented claims about machine intelligence.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The main caution is historical translation. Turing’s framework predates current neural language models by decades, so any direct application to LLMs must be interpretive rather than literal. The open question is which parts of the early machine-intelligence program still illuminate language-model debates and which belong to a very different technical world.
