## Summary
Steven T. Piantadosi argues that contemporary language models put pressure on strong anti-statistical claims in theoretical linguistics, especially claims that human-like linguistic generalization cannot arise from distribution-sensitive learning. The source is best treated as a position statement in the [[../overviews/Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models Overview|Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models]] (strand 4) dispute rather than as a settled resolution of it. Its central contribution for WoLaLa is to force the question of what exactly current model success does and does not show about grammar, abstraction, and the adequacy of statistical learning.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models Overview|Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models]] and also touches [[../overviews/Critical and Skeptical Perspectives Overview|Critical and Skeptical Perspectives]]. It represents one pole of the current disagreement over what LLMs imply for linguistic theory: whether model performance should be read as evidence against strong Chomskyan skepticism about data-driven learning. It is especially useful alongside the existing deep material on probing, distributional semantics, and linguistic competence because it turns those scattered evidential threads into an explicit theoretical confrontation.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The title-level claim is intentionally strong, so the main caution is interpretive. Even if the paper presents substantial evidence, the inference from model success to a refutation of a broad linguistic tradition is larger than any single benchmark or modeling result can bear on its own. The corresponding reply literature is therefore essential context rather than optional background.
