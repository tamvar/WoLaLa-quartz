## Summary
Murphy, Leivada, Dentella, Gunther, and Marcus argue that OpenAI's `o3` model fails on several tests aimed at hierarchical syntactic and compositional structure, even when it succeeds on simpler surface-pattern tasks. The paper's central claim is not that language models are useless, but that success on easier tasks does not justify treating current systems as if they had mastered the fundamental linguistic structures that theoretical debates care about. For WoLaLa, it is a focused anti-overclaim source at the intersection of criticism and linguistic theory.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source matters because it restates a familiar theoretical complaint in a very current form: surface-level success does not automatically establish hierarchical structural competence. It should therefore be read as a methodological warning about evidence standards, not as a blanket rejection of model progress.

## Key Points
- The paper reports failures on tests aimed at phrase structure, acceptability, and compositional relations.
- It contrasts those failures with better performance on simpler linear tasks.
- The authors use the results to push back against claims that current LLMs make linguistics obsolete.
- The article targets overinterpretation of results more than it targets deep learning as such.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The paper is closely tied to one model family and one test suite. The remaining question is whether later models can improve in a way that supports stronger structural claims rather than only better task adaptation.
