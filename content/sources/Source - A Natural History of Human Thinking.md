## Summary
Michael Tomasello argues that distinctively human thinking grows out of increasingly complex forms of cooperation. Treated selectively here, the book's central WoLaLa-relevant thesis is the shared intentionality hypothesis: humans move from individual intentionality to joint and then collective intentionality, and those shifts make possible perspective-taking, socially recursive inference, and norm-governed self-monitoring. Tomasello's opening chapter presents human thought as socially infused rather than merely solitary, and the book's overall line is that language and culture emerge from preexisting needs to coordinate attention, action, and reasons with others. This makes the work a strong cognition bridge for WoLaLa, especially where debates about language models risk sliding from linguistic performance to richer claims about human-like thought.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives Overview|Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Historical Perspectives on Language, Mind, and Modeling Overview|Historical Perspectives on Language, Mind, and Modeling]]. It matters because it gives a clear alternative to any picture that treats language-like competence as the whole story about mind. Tomasello's framework helps articulate why reasoning, perspective coordination, and norm-sensitive reflection are not straightforwardly captured by linguistic fluency alone. For WoLaLa, the book is especially useful as background for separating formal language success from the broader social-cognitive architecture of human thinking.

## Limitation Or Open Question
This is an evolutionary-cognitive synthesis rather than a source about language models themselves, and some of its reconstruction is necessarily speculative. The open question is which parts of Tomasello's social-cognitive account are best used as contrastive background for LLM debates, and which would require much more direct comparative evidence.
