## Summary
Xiaojuan Tang, Zilong Zheng, Jiaqi Li, Fanxu Meng, Song-Chun Zhu, Yitao Liang, and Muhan Zhang argue that large language models perform much of their in-context reasoning by leaning on semantic associations rather than on robust symbolic rule manipulation. Their experiments decouple semantics from reasoning tasks and find that performance degrades sharply when commonsense semantic support is removed. For WoLaLa, the paper is useful because it sharpens a key distinction: impressive reasoning performance may still be semantically scaffolded in ways that complicate stronger claims about formal or abstract reasoning.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Critical and Skeptical Perspectives Overview|Critical and Skeptical Perspectives]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Meaning, Reference, and Distributional Language Overview|Meaning, Reference, and Distributional Language]]. It is important because it pushes back against reading few-shot reasoning success as evidence of general symbolic competence. The paper instead suggests that meaning-like semantic regularities may be doing much of the work, which makes it relevant both to reasoning skepticism and to questions about what sort of semantic structure LLMs actually possess.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The paper targets a specific contrast between semantic scaffolding and symbolic reasoning, not the entire space of LLM reasoning abilities. The open question is how far its conclusions extend beyond the studied tasks and prompt settings.
