## Summary
Richard Futrell, Laura Stearns, Daniel L. Everett, Steven T. Piantadosi, and Edward Gibson revisit the famous claim that Pirahã lacks syntactic embedding and therefore challenges strong universality claims about recursion. Using corpus evidence, they argue that Pirahã does show embedding-like structures, which weakens the strongest anti-recursion reading of the language. For WoLaLa, the paper matters as a bounded empirical intervention in a long-running theoretical dispute. ## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is useful because it keeps a famous theoretical controversy tied to its actual empirical question. The debate here is not whether one language settles all issues about language, thought, or LLMs. It is whether strong claims about the absence of recursion or embedding in Pirahã survive closer corpus scrutiny. In that sense, the paper exemplifies a broader WoLaLa lesson: large theoretical arguments often depend on surprisingly specific linguistic evidence.

The source also matters because Pirahã has often been recruited into debates about universals, learnability, and the limits of formal grammar. A more cautious empirical reassessment helps prevent those broader debates from leaning on an over-simplified premise.

## Limitation Or Open Question
This is a bounded empirical paper, not a general theory of recursion or universal grammar. Even if its corpus evidence supports embedding in Pirahã, wider questions about the explanatory role of recursion, the architecture of grammar, or the implications for language models remain open.
