## Summary
Futrell et al. use controlled psycholinguistic experiments to ask whether neural language models represent syntactic state over incremental processing. The paper matters for WoLaLa because it explicitly treats language models as experimental subjects, creating a bridge between NLP evaluation and psycholinguistic method.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Linguistic Competence and Limitations Overview|Linguistic Competence and Limitations]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives Overview|Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives]]. Its main significance is methodological. Rather than asking only whether a model gets the right answer, it asks whether the model behaves as if it maintains syntactic state in ways comparable to psycholinguistic expectations. That makes it an important bridge between competence evidence and human-language processing comparison.

## Limitation Or Open Question
Treating models as psycholinguistic subjects is illuminating but also risky. Similar behavior under controlled contrasts does not by itself show human-like mechanisms or representations. The open question is how strong a bridge this experimental analogy really provides.
