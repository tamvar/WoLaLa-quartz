## Summary
Ewa Dąbrowska argues that Universal Grammar is both conceptually unstable and empirically weak. The paper reviews three standard lines of argument for UG, universality, convergence, and poverty of the stimulus, and argues that all three fail once one takes crosslinguistic diversity, individual differences, and construction-based learning seriously. For WoLaLa, the source matters because it gives a concise, source-grounded anti-UG position that remains directly relevant to language-model debates about learnability and linguistic theory.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is valuable as a compact theoretical reply note. It gives a clear statement of why diversity, non-convergence, and learnability from richer input challenge strong innate-grammar claims.

## Key Points
- Dąbrowska argues that there is little agreement on what Universal Grammar actually contains.
- The paper challenges universality arguments by emphasizing how little true crosslinguistic universality there is.
- It challenges convergence arguments by pointing to substantial differences among adult native speakers' grammatical knowledge.
- It argues that poverty-of-the-stimulus reasoning depends on assuming specifically generative-style target representations.
- For WoLaLa, the source is useful because it makes anti-UG reasoning explicit without collapsing into generic anti-theory rhetoric.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The paper is polemical in a disciplined way: it is mainly a critique of UG arguments rather than a full positive theory of language learning. Its role here is to sharpen the anti-nativist side of the debate.
