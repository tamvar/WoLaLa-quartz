## Summary
Piantadosi and Jacobs argue that the probabilistic language of thought provides a productive middle ground across several long-running divides in cognitive science. They highlight four advantages: it reframes the symbols-versus-statistics debate, mediates between nativist and empiricist learning pictures, explains the emergence of novel computational concepts, and offers a route from sensation to abstraction. For WoLaLa, the paper is a concise statement of why structured probabilistic cognition remains attractive as an explanatory program.

Its importance is strategic rather than empirical. The article shows why the probabilistic language-of-thought approach is appealing precisely because it avoids simple oppositions: not symbols or statistics, not nativism or empiricism, not sensation or abstraction. That makes it a useful compact bridge source when WoLaLa needs a clear statement of the structured-probabilistic alternative to both classic symbolic rigidity and undifferentiated statistical learning.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The article is short and programmatic. It sketches the explanatory appeal of the approach more than it proves its superiority on every case. The open question is how far this framework can scale toward the kinds of large, language-rich systems that now dominate AI discussion.
