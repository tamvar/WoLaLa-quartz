## Summary
Damián Blasi, Antonios Anastasopoulos, and Graham Neubig quantify how unevenly language technology serves the world's languages. The paper surveys NLP research coverage and performance across both user-facing tasks and foundational tasks, then ties those disparities to broader societal and academic conditions. For WoLaLa, the source matters because it gives the applications strand a strong language-equity anchor: evaluation cannot count as good language technology practice if it systematically ignores most of the world's linguistic diversity.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is important because it turns `language coverage` from a vague fairness concern into a measurable methodological problem for NLP and language-model practice.

## Key Points
- The paper measures global disparities in NLP attention and performance across thousands of languages.
- It examines both user-facing tasks and foundational tasks rather than treating one benchmark family as representative.
- The authors connect technical inequality to broader societal and academic factors and offer policy-oriented recommendations.
- The source is directly relevant to language-centered best practice because it asks who current systems actually serve.
- For WoLaLa, it strengthens the applications strand by tying evaluation methodology to linguistic diversity and scholarly responsibility.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The paper is a broad field-level snapshot rather than a detailed guide to one workflow. Its main value here is to set a standard for what language-aware methodological responsibility should include.
