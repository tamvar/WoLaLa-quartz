## Summary
Erika Petersen and Christopher Potts argue that large language models can be useful tools for lexical-semantic research, and they demonstrate this with a case study of the English verb *break*. Their central claim is not merely that LLMs encode useful similarity structure, but that they can help recover known sense distinctions and suggest new theoretically interesting combinations for analysis. For WoLaLa, the paper matters because it gives a concrete example of LLMs supporting traditional lexical-semantics inquiry without displacing it.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is useful because it moves beyond general claims about semantics and shows one precise way LLMs can interface with established lexical-semantic theory.

## Key Points
- The paper uses the polysemous verb *break* as a theory-facing lexical-semantics case study.
- It argues that LLMs capture known sense distinctions and can suggest informative new sense combinations.
- The authors emphasize continuity with lexical-semantics traditions that treat lexical meaning as high-dimensional and contextually modulated.
- They also note a genuine difference: LLMs lack the discrete feature structure often assumed in generative lexical semantics.
- For WoLaLa, the source is valuable as a constructive example of theory-guided semantic use of LLMs.

## Limitation Or Open Question
This is a bounded case study rather than a general account of semantics. Its significance lies in showing how LLMs can support lexical-semantic inquiry, not in proving that they have full semantic understanding.
