# Source - Meaning as Use from Wittgenstein to Google's Word2vec

## Summary
Ines Skelac and Andrej Jandrić read Word2vec through Frege, Wittgenstein, and Firth. Their central claim is that Word2vec inherits an important context-based picture of meaning, but implements only a much narrower notion of `use` than Wittgenstein’s later language-game view.

## Key Points
- Source fact: the chapter explicitly links Word2vec to Frege’s context principle and to Wittgenstein’s claim that words have meaning only in use and in context.
- Source fact: it also connects contemporary vector semantics to Firth’s contextual view of lexical meaning.
- Source fact: the authors argue that Word2vec captures context primarily through nearby surrounding words in sentences.
- Source fact: they contrast this with Wittgenstein’s later notion of language games, where meaning depends on broader social practices, forms of life, and non-linguistic activities.
- Source fact: the chapter concludes that Word2vec offers a partial implementation of meaning-as-use rather than a full realization of Wittgenstein’s position.

## Details
The chapter begins from the idea that meaning cannot be assigned to isolated words apart from their context. It traces this through Frege’s context principle, Wittgenstein’s early insistence that words have meaning only in propositions, and Wittgenstein’s later shift toward language games and forms of life.

Against that background, the authors present Word2vec as a philosophically interesting computational realization of contextual meaning. In Word2vec, meanings are represented by vectors learned from surrounding words, so the model operationalizes a local, sentence-level notion of use.

The chapter’s main distinction is that this is not yet Wittgenstein’s full later picture. For Wittgenstein, use is embedded in shared practices, institutions, reactions, and non-linguistic activities. Word2vec therefore captures one thin slice of use, not the entire practical and social horizon in which meaning lives.

## Synthesis / Interpretation
This source is the clearest direct bridge between Wittgensteinian meaning-as-use language and contemporary vector semantics. It strengthens the distributional side of the debate, but also sharpens one of its key limits: contextual embedding is not automatically the same thing as meaning in the richer language-game sense.

That makes it a natural follow-on to [[Source - Distributional Models of Word Meaning|Lenci]] and [[Source - Distributional Semantics|Lenci and Sahlgren]], while also clarifying why critics remain resistant to quick upgrades from contextual success to full understanding.
