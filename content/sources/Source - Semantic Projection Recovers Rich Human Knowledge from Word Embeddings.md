## Summary
Gabriel Grand, Idan Asher Blank, Francisco Pereira, and Evelina Fedorenko argue that word embeddings encode much richer context-dependent world knowledge than simple overall similarity measures suggest. Their `semantic projection` method recovers human judgments about features such as size, danger, and habitat by projecting embeddings onto interpretable semantic dimensions. For WoLaLa, the paper matters because it offers a precise bridge from distributional representations to human-like feature judgments without claiming that embeddings thereby solve all questions about meaning.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is useful because it links distributional representation to human judgment structure in an interpretable way, making it a strong bridge between semantic representation and cognitive comparison.

## Key Points
- The paper proposes `semantic projection` as a simple geometrical method for extracting feature-specific knowledge from word embeddings.
- It argues that embeddings represent a wealth of context-dependent knowledge beyond undifferentiated similarity.
- The method recovers human judgments across different object categories and properties.
- The source is a constructive bridge between distributional semantics and cognitively interpretable feature structure.
- For WoLaLa, it helps clarify what can be recovered from embeddings without leaping directly to full understanding claims.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The paper is about recoverable feature knowledge in embeddings, not about grounded reference or full semantic competence. It shows a strong representational result, but not a complete theory of meaning.
