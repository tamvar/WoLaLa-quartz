## Summary
J. C. Nyiri's 1989 article approaches machine consciousness through Wittgenstein's later philosophy, especially the shift toward meaning as use and the anti-mentalism and anti-essentialism associated with *Philosophical Investigations*. The opening of the paper makes clear that Nyiri is not treating consciousness as a hidden inner essence that could be settled by abstract definition alone. Instead, he frames the issue through Wittgensteinian worries about how mental predicates, thought, and meaning gain their significance within public practices and forms of life. For WoLaLa, the article matters as a historical skeptical bridge: it helps place contemporary claims about machine understanding or machine mindedness against an older line of thought that resists turning mental vocabulary into a purely internal or purely symbolic matter.

## Limitation Or Open Question
This is a late-1980s philosophical article, not a discussion of current language models, and this brief is intentionally narrow: it relies on the verified publication record and the paper's explicit opening framing rather than pretending to summarize every step of the argument. The open question is how directly Wittgensteinian arguments about public criteria and meaning-as-use should bear on present LLM debates about understanding, consciousness, or agency.
