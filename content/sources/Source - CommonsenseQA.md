## Summary
Alon Talmor, Jonathan Herzig, Nicholas Lourie, and Jonathan Berant's "CommonsenseQA: A Question Answering Challenge Targeting Commonsense Knowledge" introduces a multiple-choice question-answering benchmark built from ConceptNet relations and designed to require commonsense knowledge rather than mere retrieval from a provided passage. For WoLaLa, the source matters as a representative case of benchmark construction: it operationalizes a cognition-facing claim through language tasks while also making clear how quickly such benchmarks can become entangled with annotation artifacts and leaderboard-focused interpretation.

## Limitation Or Open Question
As a benchmark paper, this source defines a task more than a theory of reasoning. It does not show what internal resources models actually use to solve the questions. The open question is how far success on such datasets reflects robust world knowledge rather than exploitable regularities in task construction and training data exposure.
