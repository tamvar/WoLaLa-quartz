## Summary
Adele E. Goldberg’s *Constructions at Work* is a usage-based linguistic foundation for thinking about generalization, productivity, and the role of constructions in language. For WoLaLa, its importance is less in any single claim about LLMs than in giving [[../overviews/Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models Overview|Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models]] (strand 4) a clearly represented non-Chomskyan theoretical background. It helps explain why some current language-model results are read not only as engineering achievements but as evidence relevant to broader debates over how language structure and generalization should be understood.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models Overview|Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models]] and provides historical-theoretical background for [[../overviews/Linguistic Competence and Limitations Overview|Linguistic Competence and Limitations]] (strand 1) and [[../overviews/Meaning, Reference, and Distributional Language Overview|Meaning, Reference, and Distributional Language]] (strand 3). It is useful because the current wiki already has deep notes on distributional semantics, lexical competence, and model behavior, but much less direct representation of constructionist or usage-based theory. A brief note here makes that position legible without forcing a large new cluster.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The main caution is temporal and methodological. This is not a source about contemporary LLMs, so its role in WoLaLa must remain inferential and comparative rather than retrospective proof that current models realize a usage-based theory. The open question is which parts of the constructionist picture genuinely map onto present model behavior and which do not.
