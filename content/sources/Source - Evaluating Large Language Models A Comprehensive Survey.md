## Summary
Wang et al. offer a panoramic survey of LLM evaluation that explicitly groups the field into knowledge-and-capability evaluation, alignment evaluation, and safety evaluation. In WoLaLa terms, it is a useful complement to [[../sources/Source - A Survey on Evaluation of Large Language Models|A Survey on Evaluation of Large Language Models]] because it is less organized around task and benchmark logistics and more around evaluation targets and risk categories.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Applications and Best Practices Overview|Applications and Best Practices]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Critical and Skeptical Perspectives Overview|Critical and Skeptical Perspectives]]. Its main payoff is contrastive. Where the Chang et al. survey asks what, where, and how to evaluate, this survey emphasizes the different normative targets of evaluation: capability, alignment, and safety. That makes it useful for WoLaLa because it clarifies that language-model evaluation always embeds assumptions about what kind of failure matters most.

## Limitation Or Open Question
Because the survey is panoramic, it can flatten important differences among narrowly linguistic, socially embedded, and alignment-focused forms of evaluation. The open question is how far these three families can really be integrated before language-centered evaluation loses specificity.
