## Summary
Ian Tenney, Patrick Xia, Berlin Chen, Alex Wang, Adam Poliak, R. Thomas McCoy, Najoung Kim, Benjamin Van Durme, Samuel R. Bowman, Dipanjan Das, and Ellie Pavlick probe contextualized word representations for sentence-structure information across several tasks. The paper's importance for WoLaLa is that it pushes probing beyond token-local tagging and into more relational linguistic structure. It therefore sits naturally in the competence strand as a bridge between simple extractability results and richer claims about contextual linguistic organization.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Linguistic Competence and Limitations Overview|Linguistic Competence and Limitations]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models Overview|Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models]]. It matters because sentence-structure probing is closer to linguistically interesting generalization than many surface-level diagnostics, but still requires caution about what probes demonstrate. The paper is therefore helpful both as evidence and as a reminder that richer tasks do not automatically remove interpretive ambiguity.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The probing framework remains diagnostic rather than decisively explanatory. The open question is how much these results reflect representations the model actively relies on, rather than information that can be recovered by powerful readout models.
