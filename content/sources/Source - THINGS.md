## Summary
Martin N. Hebart, Adam H. Dickter, Alexis Kidder, Wan Y. Kwok, Anna Corriveau, Caitlin Van Wicklin, and Chris I. Baker introduce THINGS, a large database of 1,854 object concepts and more than 26,000 naturalistic object images, along with category metadata and validation against semantic embeddings and convolutional neural-network representations. For WoLaLa, the source matters because it provides a disciplined concept-and-image resource that can support human-model comparison rather than only free-form benchmark chasing.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Neuroscientific Perspectives Overview|Neuroscientific Perspectives]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives Overview|Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives]]. It is useful because it supplies a shared object-concept inventory with naturalistic images, human category structure, and neural-network validation. That makes it a potential bridge resource for comparing semantic organization across behavioral, computational, and neuroscientific settings.

## Limitation Or Open Question
THINGS is a resource paper, not a theory of representation. The open question is how far such concept-image databases can ground strong claims about semantic structure in models or brains rather than mainly improving measurement and comparability.
