## Summary
Walid S. Saba's "Language, logic and ontology: uncovering the structure of commonsense knowledge" argues that commonsense ontology should be discovered from the structure of natural language rather than imposed in an ad hoc way from abstract formal systems alone. The paper treats language as a guide to the ontological categories that commonsense reasoning presupposes, and it proposes a strongly typed background ontology for semantic interpretation. For WoLaLa, the source matters as a bridge between formal semantics, commonsense reasoning, and current questions about whether language models capture structured meaning or merely predictive regularities.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is useful as a compact bridge note. It gives a strong alternative to views that treat semantics as either detached symbolic bookkeeping or as nothing more than statistical usage regularity. Saba instead argues that natural language encodes clues to a richly typed commonsense ontology.

## Key Points
- The paper argues that the structure of commonsense knowledge should be discovered rather than freely invented.
- Natural language is treated as evidence about the ontology required for semantic interpretation.
- Saba proposes a strongly typed ontological background as a way to make semantic analysis more nearly trivial once the right types are in place.
- The paper uses this program to approach lexical ambiguity, intensionality, and nominal compounds.
- Its value for WoLaLa is less that it settles current LLM debates than that it sharpens the contrast between shallow predictive success and meaning grounded in structured commonsense categories.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The paper is an ambitious programmatic proposal rather than a direct empirical test of language models. Its importance here is conceptual and methodological: it clarifies what a strongly structured semantics-and-ontology alternative would look like.
