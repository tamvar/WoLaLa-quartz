## Summary
de Marneffe, Manning, Nivre, and Zeman present Universal Dependencies as both a crosslinguistically consistent annotation framework and a linguistic theory of morphosyntactic structure organized around words, heads, dependents, and grammatical relations. For WoLaLa, the article matters because it supplies a practical and theoretical bridge between typologically oriented linguistics and computational language analysis, especially for work that evaluates or compares model structure across languages.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is useful because UD is not merely an engineering format. The paper explicitly argues that grammatical relations and crosslinguistically comparable morphosyntactic analysis can support both NLP and broader linguistic inquiry. That makes it a good methodology bridge whenever model evaluation depends on parsed corpora, dependency relations, or multilingual structural comparison.

## Key Points
- UD is presented as a coherent linguistic framework, not just an annotation compromise.
- The framework emphasizes words and grammatical relations as core building blocks.
- It aims at crosslinguistic consistency across typologically diverse languages.
- The source is especially relevant for methodology because many later evaluation and probing results rely on UD-style corpora or assumptions.
