## Summary
Luciano Floridi argues that systems such as ChatGPT display a significant form of agency without thereby exhibiting intelligence, thought, or understanding in the richer human sense. His central claim is that LLMs can manipulate the formal structure of language with extraordinary success while remaining noncognitive in the stronger semantic sense. For WoLaLa, the editorial matters because it provides a compact SSH-facing vocabulary for separating successful linguistic action from intelligence attribution.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives Overview|Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Mind Design and Reverse Engineering Overview|Mind Design and Reverse Engineering]]. It is useful because it resists a familiar binary. Floridi does not say that LLMs are trivial, but he also insists that formal text-processing success should not be mistaken for intelligence or human-like semantic understanding. The notion of agency without intelligence therefore offers a compact interpretive category for discussing systems that can do socially and epistemically important things with language while remaining noncognitive in a stronger sense.

## Limitation Or Open Question
This is a short editorial intervention rather than a developed empirical argument. Its value is conceptual framing. The open question is whether the category of agency without intelligence can remain stable as multimodal and tool-using systems become more interactive and world-linked.
