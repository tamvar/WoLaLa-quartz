## Summary
This March 2023 essay by Noam Chomsky, Ian Roberts, and Jeffrey Watumull argues that current large language models are powerful pattern-completion systems but not explanations of language, knowledge, or mind. It sharply contrasts probabilistic prediction with explanation-oriented science and presents the public-facing anti-LLM case in compressed form. For WoLaLa, the piece matters mainly as a historically visible framing document rather than as a technical argument.

## WoLaLa Relevance
The essay condenses several recurring skeptical themes into one prominent intervention: prediction is not explanation, statistical fit is not understanding, and science should seek the principles behind human knowledge rather than only outward imitation.

## Key Points
- The essay contrasts prediction-driven engineering success with explanation-oriented science.
- It treats LLMs as technologically impressive but conceptually shallow.
- It presents a public version of the claim that language and cognition require richer underlying principles.
- It became one of the best-known broad-readership statements of the anti-LLM position.

## Limitation Or Open Question
As a newspaper essay, the piece is rhetorically compressed and not intended as a detailed scholarly exchange. Its main value is diagnostic and historical.
