## Summary
Michael Tomasello argues that human communication is grounded first in cooperative social interaction rather than in an autonomous language faculty understood apart from shared activity. Treated selectively here, the book's core WoLaLa-relevant claim is that pointing, pantomiming, shared intentionality, and common ground provide the psychological infrastructure from which conventional language and later grammatical structuring arise. Tomasello's opening chapters frame human communication as a cooperative enterprise oriented toward requesting, informing, and sharing; later chapters, especially the grammatical one, argue that different communicative motives create different pressures for more elaborate conventions and constructions. The result is a strongly usage- and interaction-centered picture of how linguistic structure emerges from social coordination rather than from a single pre-packaged system.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives Overview|Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models Overview|Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models]]. It matters because it offers a strong counterpoint to language-first or syntax-first pictures of linguistic competence. For WoLaLa, Tomasello helps connect language-model debates to broader questions about whether linguistic structure should be explained through cooperative use, cultural conventionalization, and shared intentionality rather than through an abstract rule system alone. The book is also useful background for why some usage-based and constructionist views see grammar as an outcome of communication, not a precondition for it.

## Limitation Or Open Question
This is a broad book on language origins and cooperative communication, not an LLM study, and this note treats it selectively rather than as a full-book summary. The open question is how far Tomasello's cooperation-first account should constrain present interpretations of models that display striking linguistic behavior without human-like social embedding.
