## Summary
Idan Blank's forum article asks a prior question that many LLM comparisons skip: when large language models are treated as models, what exactly are they supposed to model? Blank distinguishes different interpretation targets, including behavior, linguistic processing, neural responses, and broader cognition. For WoLaLa, the piece matters because it forces more precision about what human-model comparison claims are actually about.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is valuable as a framing note. It helps keep behavioral similarity, linguistic competence, neural alignment, and cognitive explanation from being casually collapsed into one claim about what LLMs are.

## Key Points
- The article distinguishes several different interpretation targets for LLM comparison.
- It argues that empirical similarity is hard to interpret without first specifying the target domain.
- Neural and cognitive modeling questions are not the same as questions about linguistic performance.
- The piece is useful because it slows down rhetorical slides from task success to human-model equivalence.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The article is brief and programmatic. It identifies the interpretive problem more clearly than it solves it.
