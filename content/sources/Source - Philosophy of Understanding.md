## Summary
"Philosophy of understanding" is a short section from the foundation-model report *On the Opportunities and Risks of Foundation Models*. It asks what it would mean for a foundation model to understand natural language and distinguishes that metaphysical question from the epistemic question of how we would tell. Its main value for WoLaLa is clarificatory: the section does not claim that current models already understand, but it argues that definitive skepticism may be premature and that debates about understanding need cleaner distinctions.

## WoLaLa Relevance

The section is useful because it explicitly separates several questions that are often collapsed: what foundation models are trained to do, what sorts of information their self-supervised regime gives them, what understanding would amount to, and why trust, interpretability, and accountability might depend on that issue. It therefore helps make discussions of understanding less rhetorical and more methodologically disciplined.

## Limitation Or Open Question
This is a compact report section, not a full monograph or a source of decisive empirical evidence. It is strongest as a framework for clarifying debate. The open question is whether its cautious openness about future understanding remains persuasive once one presses harder on grounding, embodiment, or causal interaction with the world.
