## Summary
Jasdeep Singh, Caiming Xiong, and Richard Socher argue against treating BERT representations as a simple language-neutral interlingua. For WoLaLa, the paper matters because it narrows a recurring temptation in multilingual model interpretation: if cross-lingual success is observed, does that mean the model has learned a shared abstract linguistic space, or only a weaker and less theory-rich kind of alignment? This makes the source a useful multilingual bridge inside the broader strand-4 question of what model representations imply for linguistic theory.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports strand 4 and secondarily strand 1. It gives the current nine-strand map a theory-relevant multilingual counterpoint without requiring a whole multilingual cluster. It is especially useful next to the probing and BERT material already in the wiki, because it turns representation analysis into a more explicit question about what counts as shared linguistic structure across languages.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The main caution is scope. This is a BERT-era multilingual representation argument, not a general verdict on all later multilingual or large-scale language models. The open question is how far its caution transfers to current transformer systems with different scales, objectives, and multilingual regimes.
