## Summary
Max Louwerse argues that symbolic and embodied accounts of cognition are often opposed too sharply because both underestimate the amount of meaning-bearing structure already present in language itself. On this view, distributional regularities in linguistic surface form encode traces of embodied and world-structured experience, so symbolic and embodied explanations are interdependent rather than simply rival. For WoLaLa, the paper is valuable as a bridge between distributional language, cognition, and model-brain debates.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives Overview|Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Neuroscientific Perspectives Overview|Neuroscientific Perspectives]]. It matters because it offers an explicit reconciliation strategy: statistical regularities in language are not merely shallow correlations but partly encode embodied structure. That makes the paper useful wherever WoLaLa asks whether distributional success should be read as evidence against embodiment, or whether linguistic and embodied information can coexist in an integrated account.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The paper is a theory-bridging argument, not direct evidence about contemporary language models. The open question is how far its symbol-interdependency proposal can be transported from latent-semantic and embodied-cognition debates into transformer-era language modeling.
