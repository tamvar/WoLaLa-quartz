## Summary
Charles Kemp and Terry Regier argue that crosslinguistic variation in kinship systems is constrained by general communicative principles. The paper treats kinship categories not as arbitrary cultural inventories but as systems shaped by pressures toward useful and learnable communication. For WoLaLa, the source matters because it offers a compact case study in how semantic and conceptual structure can be explained through communicative optimization rather than by appeal to a fixed universal inventory alone.

This source is useful as a bounded cultural-cognitive case study. It shows how crosslinguistic semantic organization can be analyzed through communicative principles, which makes it relevant to broader WoLaLa questions about efficiency, conceptual structure, and language as socially shaped.

## Key Points
- The paper argues that kinship-category systems vary, but within constraints shaped by communicative usefulness.
- It presents crosslinguistic semantic organization as responsive to general communicative principles rather than arbitrary cultural noise.
- The source is a helpful example of how language structure can be explained at an intermediate level between pure universals and unconstrained variation.
- It strengthens the strand's SSH side by using a culturally rich domain rather than a narrow formal benchmark.
- For WoLaLa, it helps keep `meaning from use` connected to human communicative organization rather than to text statistics alone.

## Limitation Or Open Question
This is a focused semantic case study, not a general theory of language or cognition. Its main contribution here is to exemplify how communication-based explanation can illuminate structured variation.
