## Summary
John Searle's *Minds, Brains and Science* restates and broadens several themes associated with his anti-strong-AI position. Based on the 1984 Reith Lectures, it places the computer-thinking question inside a wider set of problems about mind, causation, cognitive science, and action. For WoLaLa, the book matters mainly as a concise historical bridge: it shows how the argument of [[../sources/Source - Minds, Brains, and Programs|Minds, Brains, and Programs]] sits inside a larger philosophical program rather than as an isolated thought experiment.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Historical Perspectives on Language, Mind, and Modeling Overview|Historical Perspectives on Language, Mind, and Modeling]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Critical and Skeptical Perspectives Overview|Critical and Skeptical Perspectives]]. It is useful as a compact statement of Searle's broader picture: minds are biological phenomena, intentionality depends on causal powers rather than formal program structure alone, and cognitive science must not confuse computational description with genuine mentality. That makes it a good supporting reference when the wiki needs the wider Searlean frame rather than only the Chinese Room.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The source is broad and lecture-like rather than tightly focused on language models or linguistic evidence. Its main value is contextual and historical. The open question is how much of Searle's biological naturalism remains diagnostic once present debates concern learned statistical systems rather than classical symbolic AI alone.
