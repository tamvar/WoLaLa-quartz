## Summary
Peter Norvig's essay responds to Chomsky's criticism of statistical language modeling by arguing that engineering success is not irrelevant to science, that probabilistic models can contribute explanatory insight even when they are hard to inspect parameter by parameter, and that natural-language interpretation is often inherently probabilistic. For WoLaLa, the essay is valuable as an early, clear statement of the statistical-learning reply to strong anti-probabilistic positions in linguistics.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This essay matters because it states a now-familiar position before the current LLM moment fully arrived: probabilistic language models may be scientifically relevant even if they do not look like concise symbolic theories. Norvig does not deny the importance of underlying principles, but he argues that fact-gathering, engineering success, and probabilistic interpretation problems all belong inside the science of language.

## Key Points
- Norvig rejects the idea that statistical modeling success is irrelevant to scientific understanding.
- He argues that language interpretation involves uncertainty and is therefore naturally open to probabilistic treatment.
- The essay pushes back on dismissing probabilistic models because early simple models were limited.
- It treats engineering progress as evidence worth taking seriously, though not as a substitute for explanation.
- The piece is useful as a historical precursor to later disputes over what LLM success does or does not show.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The essay is polemical and programmatic rather than a detailed empirical study. Its value lies in clarifying the dispute. The open question is how far probabilistic success can go before one still needs stronger theory about linguistic structure, cognition, or meaning.
