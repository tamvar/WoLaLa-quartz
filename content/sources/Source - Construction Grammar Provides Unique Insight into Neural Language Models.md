## Summary
Leonie Weissweiler, Taiqi He, Naoki Otani, David R. Mortensen, Lori Levin, and Hinrich Schütze argue that construction grammar offers a distinctive lens for probing language models. Their position paper claims that language models must represent constructions as pairings of form and meaning, including non-contiguous and partially filled patterns, if they are to count as serious models of language. For WoLaLa, the paper matters because it connects an explicitly usage-based theoretical framework to current probing and model-analysis practice.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is useful as a bridge note from constructionist linguistic theory to current model-analysis methodology. It helps show why some theory-facing questions are missed by syntax-only or token-level probing.

## Key Points
- The paper argues that language models must capture constructions as joint form-meaning pairings, not only isolated words or simple dependencies.
- Construction grammar is presented as a way to test whether models encode abstract, partially filled, and non-contiguous linguistic patterns.
- The authors treat current probing methods as inadequate for many constructional questions and call for more appropriate methodology and better annotated data.
- The paper links theory choice to practical model evaluation: if models miss constructional organization, downstream success may conceal important linguistic weaknesses.
- For WoLaLa, it broadens the theory-facing bridge beyond generative-versus-statistical disputes by adding a concrete constructionist alternative.

## Limitation Or Open Question
This is a position paper rather than a full empirical demonstration. Its main value is to define a promising testing agenda and to explain why construction-level evaluation should matter.
