## Summary
John Haugeland's *Artificial Intelligence: The Very Idea* reconstructs and evaluates the classical AI program he calls GOFAI: the empirical hypothesis that intelligence is realized through rational, quasi-linguistic symbol manipulation. The book explains why that hypothesis is powerful without treating either its success or failure as settled. Its most direct WoLaLa contribution is a historically grounded account of why semantics, pragmatic language use, common sense, and person-level understanding are difficult tests for computational theories of mind.

## WoLaLa Relevance
Haugeland treats AI as an attempt to build genuine minds, not merely useful imitations, and traces its appeal to the idea that thinking and computing share an abstract symbolic organization. He then develops the machinery needed to assess that idea: automatic formal systems, interpretation, original and derivative meaning, computer architectures, knowledge representation, and the practical record of classical AI.

Language is central to the assessment rather than a decorative example. The book argues that linguistic competence includes pragmatic action, conversational cooperation, metacognition, and the ability to make coherent sense across word and deed. Its discussions of machine translation, microworlds, commonsense knowledge, the frame problem, and ordinary narrative understanding show why formal success in a restricted domain need not scale to language use in an open world.

This makes the book a useful historical bridge for present disputes about whether fluent model behavior is merely behavioral, genuinely explanatory, or mind-relevant. The bridge must remain cautious: Haugeland is evaluating 1980s symbolic cognitive science, not contemporary neural language models.

## Key Points

- GOFAI is presented as a substantive empirical hypothesis, not as an inevitable consequence of computation or a thesis that can be refuted by definition.
- Symbol manipulation is medium-independent, but computation still depends on an interpretation under which formal activity makes coherent sense.
- The book distinguishes derivative symbol meaning from the unresolved problem of original meaning and asks whether semantic activity can support the latter.
- Classical AI's successes in games, restricted microworlds, and expert domains do not by themselves solve machine translation, common sense, relevance, or the frame problem.
- Ordinary linguistic understanding draws on pragmatic purpose, conversational implicature, metacognition, affect, and personal involvement, making person-level competence a more demanding target than formal sentence processing alone.
- Haugeland rejects both easy a priori debunking and confident boosterism. He concludes that GOFAI might be right, partly right, or wrong, while the hard questions remain open.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The main caution is temporal and architectural distance. The book assesses the symbolic GOFAI tradition through the mid-1980s; it does not adjudicate whether transformer systems fit, revive, or escape those categories. Its questions about interpretation, pragmatic coherence, common sense, and person-level understanding remain relevant, but applying its verdicts directly to LLMs would require a separate argument.
