## Summary
John Haugeland’s *Artificial Intelligence: The Very Idea* is a compact historical-philosophical anchor for disputes about what artificial intelligence is trying to explain, simulate, or instantiate. For WoLaLa, its central contribution is not a direct claim about contemporary language models, but a framing of the ambitions and conceptual stakes behind AI projects that aim at minded or language-capable behavior. It therefore helps connect current debates over LLM understanding and reverse engineering to earlier questions about representation, cognition, and explanatory aspiration.

It gives the historical overview a clearer backbone than a bare title list, and it helps the reverse-engineering strand situate contemporary mechanism stories within older debates about what would count as artificial intelligence in the strong sense. It is especially useful as background for reading present-day disagreements over whether language-model success is merely behavioral, genuinely explanatory, or somehow mind-relevant.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The main caution is temporal and conceptual distance. This source clarifies the older intellectual frame, but it does not by itself adjudicate how well today’s transformer systems fit, revive, or escape those earlier categories. The open question is which parts of Haugeland’s framing remain genuinely diagnostic for LLM-era debates.
