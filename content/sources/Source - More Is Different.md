## Summary
Philip W. Anderson's classic essay argues that reductionism does not license the stronger constructionist assumption that higher-level sciences can be straightforwardly derived from lower-level laws. As systems become larger and more complex, new organizational properties emerge, and understanding them requires new concepts, models, and research programs at their own level. For WoLaLa, the paper is a compact but powerful historical anchor for arguments about emergence, levels of explanation, and why success at one level need not eliminate the need for higher-level theory.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source matters because many present disputes about language models oscillate between two extremes: either lower-level mechanism explains everything, or higher-level explanatory categories are empty. Anderson gives a disciplined alternative. One can accept reductionism in the weak sense that everything obeys physical law while still insisting that explanation at higher levels is indispensable because complexity brings genuinely new organizational structure into view.

## Key Points
- The paper distinguishes reductionism from the stronger claim that all higher-level explanation is dispensable.
- New properties emerge at higher levels of complexity and require their own theoretical treatment.
- Anderson's argument is historical and methodological, not a rejection of lower-level science.
- The source is valuable for WoLaLa because it clarifies why linguistic, cognitive, and functional explanation need not be replaced by lower-level model description alone.
