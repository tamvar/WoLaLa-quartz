## Summary
Kocijan and colleagues review the Winograd Schema Challenge literature as of 2020, surveying datasets, system designs, and the challenge's role as an attempted alternative to the Turing test. The paper is useful because it does not treat Winograd-style performance as self-interpreting evidence of understanding. Instead, it maps the task family, how models approach it, and why the benchmark became a focal point in arguments about commonsense and language understanding.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source matters as a bridge between benchmark history and stronger claims about understanding. For WoLaLa, it helps explain why Winograd-style tasks became attractive, why they were overread, and why later critiques of such benchmarks were methodologically important rather than merely negative.

## Key Points
- The paper surveys Winograd-schema datasets and model approaches rather than defending one system.
- It clarifies the benchmark's original motivation as a challenge aimed at commonsense reasoning and pronoun resolution.
- The review shows how quickly benchmark-specific methods and dataset artifacts became part of the story.
- It is useful background for understanding why later work treated Winograd success as weaker evidence than initially hoped.

## Limitation Or Open Question
As a 2020 review, the paper captures a moving target before the benchmark's later decline in status became clearer. The remaining question is what should replace such tasks when the aim is to evaluate language-linked commonsense without encouraging narrow overfitting.
