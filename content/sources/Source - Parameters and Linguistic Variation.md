## Summary

Michelle Sheehan traces the parameter from GB’s finite choice points through lexical and microparametric theories to proposals that locate variation at phonological form. Using word order and argument expression, she shows both the explanatory appeal and the instability of parameter formats. The enduring achievement is not a settled list of switches but a research program that seeks structured relations among surface differences and tests how complex grammars can be acquired from limited evidence.

## Evolving Parameter Concepts

In early P&P, a learner sets a finite inventory of UG-given parameters. A setting can determine a cluster of consequences, allowing evidence for one surface property to support knowledge of others. The pro-drop parameter was a paradigm case: subject omission, agreement, inversion, and extraction were analyzed as related outcomes rather than separately learned facts.

Minimalist work reduces the amount of variation attributed to narrow syntax. On the Borer-style view often associated with Chomsky, variation lies in features of functional elements. Microparametric approaches pursue fine-grained lexical or constructional differences; macroparametric approaches retain broad typological types; parameter hierarchies allow local choices to generate wider implicational tendencies. Some proposals push word-order variation into externalization or PF, leaving a more uniform syntax.

## Case Studies and Empirical Pressure

The head-directionality tradition tries to explain correlations among verb–object, adposition–complement, and other orders. Work on Universal 20 and the Final-over-Final Condition instead asks why some sequences are frequent, rare, or unattested, and whether syntactic restrictions, processing, learning biases, or historical accident provide the best explanation.

Argument expression supplies another test. Null-subject systems vary beyond a binary pro-drop setting, motivating distinctions among consistent, partial, discourse, and radical pro-drop and analyses involving agreement, pronoun morphology, deletion, and information structure. These cases show why early parameters splintered, but also why a theory cannot simply ignore the dependencies uncovered.

Sheehan treats revision as empirical progress. Competing parameter formulations have generated detailed generalizations and eliminated inadequate analyses. The challenge for strong Minimalism and nongenerative theories alike is to explain the remaining patterns without merely simplifying them away.

## WoLaLa Interpretation

Parameter theory offers a precise standard for claims that language models discover linguistic variation. A model should not count as learning a parameter merely because it performs differently across languages. Evidence should test whether one learned distinction predicts a coherent cluster of effects, how exceptions are represented, and whether the pattern transfers to sparse or unseen cases.

The chapter also highlights alternative explanations. A crosslinguistic regularity might arise from shared syntax, externalization, processing, acquisition bias, frequency, or diachrony. Model behavior can inform the debate only when experiments distinguish these sources.

## Limitations

- No consensus exists on parameter granularity, location, or learning mechanism.
- Several proposed universals have exceptions or competing functional and historical explanations.
- The chapter defends the empirical productivity of the program more strongly than any single implementation.
- It does not analyze current neural language models directly.
