## Summary
Cameron R. Jones and Benjamin K. Bergen report controlled, preregistered three-party Turing tests in which GPT-4.5 and LLaMa-3.1 variants were judged human at high rates under persona prompts. For WoLaLa, the paper matters less as a proof of understanding than as a clear behavioral substitutability result. It gives current debates a concrete empirical case where conversational imitation becomes socially significant even if deeper questions about meaning, cognition, or consciousness remain unsettled.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Critical and Skeptical Perspectives Overview|Critical and Skeptical Perspectives]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives Overview|Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives]]. It is useful because it helps separate several claims that are often conflated: passing a behavioral substitutability test, understanding language, having human-like cognition, and being a trustworthy social agent. The paper strengthens one of those claims while leaving the others open.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The paper explicitly measures judged humanness in a tightly designed conversational setting, not semantic grounding or human-like cognition in general. The open question is how much theoretical weight should be placed on Turing-style substitutability once persona prompting and deception-sensitive framing are taken into account.
