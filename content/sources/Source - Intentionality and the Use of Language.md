## Summary
John R. Searle distinguishes logical `intensionality` from mental `intentionality` and argues that linguistic phenomena derive their significance from the intentional states and practices of language users rather than from formal sentence properties alone. For WoLaLa, the paper matters because it sharpens a classic distinction behind current disputes over whether formal or distributional success can amount to meaning, reference, or understanding.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is a compact philosophy-of-language bridge. It helps distinguish sentence-level formal properties from the richer intentional structures often invoked when people claim that a system really means or understands something.

## Key Points
- Searle distinguishes sentence-level intensionality from mind-level intentionality.
- He argues that the meaning-bearing role of language depends on intentional agents and their practices, not on formal structure alone.
- The paper is useful for separating logical substitution tests from broader questions about aboutness and meaning.
- It provides historical background for later skeptical arguments about artificial systems and understanding.
- For WoLaLa, it is valuable because LLM debates often slide between formal-semantic and intentional notions without keeping them distinct.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The paper is conceptual rather than empirical. Its main value here is to make a distinction that later semantic and AI debates often blur.
