## Summary
Goodman, Tenenbaum, and Gerstenberg present concepts as part of a probabilistic language of thought: a combinatorial representational system whose outputs support graded expectations under uncertainty. The chapter argues that human knowledge is both productive and probabilistic, and that concepts should therefore be modeled as structured representations linked to probability distributions over world states. For WoLaLa, the source is useful as a compact bridge between symbolic compositionality and probabilistic inference.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives Overview|Cultural, Cognitive, and SSH Perspectives]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Mind Design and Reverse Engineering Overview|Mind Design and Reverse Engineering]]. It matters because it offers a middle position between bare statistical association and rigid symbolic hand-coding. Concepts are treated as compositional and abstract, but also graded and uncertainty-sensitive. That makes the chapter a useful reference when WoLaLa sources ask how language-capable intelligence could combine structured representations with probabilistic learning and inference.

## Limitation Or Open Question
This is a conceptual and modeling chapter, not direct evidence about language models. Its value lies in offering a framework. The open question is how far a probabilistic language-of-thought approach can be related to large neural systems without losing the interpretability and compositional structure that motivate it.
