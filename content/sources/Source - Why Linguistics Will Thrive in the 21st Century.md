## Summary
Kodner, Payne, and Heinz reply directly to strong claims that large language models refute Chomskyan linguistics. They argue that current models still learn from radically different input conditions than children, that prediction machines are not by themselves scientific theories of human language, and that evaluating LLM achievements already presupposes an independent theory of human linguistic capacity. For WoLaLa, the paper is an explicit theory-facing counterposition that keeps the current LLM debate tied to older questions about explanation, learnability, and cognitive relevance.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is important because it states the anti-overreach reply to Piantadosi-style arguments in a clean, current form. It does not deny that LLMs are useful or impressive. Instead, it argues that their success does not yet solve the central acquisition problem, does not by itself constitute an interpretable scientific theory of language, and still relies on linguistics to specify what human linguistic capacities are.

## Key Points
- The paper argues that LLM training conditions differ too sharply from child acquisition to settle learnability debates straightforwardly.
- It insists that predictive success is not the same as explanatory adequacy.
- Linguistics is presented as indispensable for defining the target capacities that model comparisons are supposed to illuminate.
- The reply is useful because it makes the theoretical disagreement explicit rather than leaving it as rhetoric around benchmark success.

## Limitation Or Open Question
This is a reply paper, so part of its structure is shaped by its target. Its value lies in stating the skeptical position clearly, not in closing the debate. The open question is which future model-learning results would genuinely force a revision of its pessimism about unconstrained learning claims.
