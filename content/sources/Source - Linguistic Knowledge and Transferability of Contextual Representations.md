## Summary
Nelson F. Liu, Matt Gardner, Yonatan Belinkov, Matthew E. Peters, and Noah A. Smith examine how much linguistic knowledge contextual representations encode and how transferable that knowledge is across tasks and pretraining regimes. The paper broadens the older probing question from "is some linguistic information extractable?" to "what kinds of information transfer, under what pretraining conditions, and with what limits?" For WoLaLa, it works as a useful bridge between representation analysis and stronger evidence standards.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Linguistic Competence and Limitations Overview|Linguistic Competence and Limitations]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models Overview|Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models]]. It matters because transferability complicates the simple claim that models "contain" linguistic knowledge. The paper helps distinguish between local extractability, broad reuse, and the specific effects of different pretraining objectives, which is exactly the sort of methodological distinction WoLaLa needs when judging competence claims.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The work is centered on contextual word representations before the current frontier LLM wave, so its direct model coverage is historically earlier. The open question is how well its lessons about transferability extend to larger instruction-tuned models and multilingual systems.
