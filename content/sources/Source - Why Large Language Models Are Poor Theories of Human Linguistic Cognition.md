## Summary
Roni Katzir argues that even strong language-model performance should not be taken straightforwardly as a good theory of human linguistic cognition. In WoLaLa terms, the source matters less as a blanket rejection of LLM relevance than as a demand for a stricter bridge between performance and cognitive explanation. It therefore functions as a direct counterweight to stronger claims that current LLMs already undermine major objections from linguistic theory.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source supports strand 4 and bridges toward strand 5. It gives the nine-strand map a clearly represented reply position in the current dispute over what LLM success means for linguistic theory. ## Limitation Or Open Question
The main caution is that a negative verdict on LLMs as theories of *human cognition* does not by itself settle what they may still show about linguistic structure, distributional generalization, or methodological challenge to older claims. The key unresolved question is therefore where to draw the boundary between cognitive inadequacy and theoretical irrelevance.
