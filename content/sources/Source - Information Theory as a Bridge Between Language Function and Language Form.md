## Summary
Richard Futrell and Michael Hahn's "Information Theory as a Bridge Between Language Function and Language Form" argues that information theory can connect formal and functional theories of language through constrained optimization. Rather than treating descriptive formalisms and communicative function as incommensurable, the paper proposes a shared mathematical space in which language structure can be derived from tradeoffs among communication, complexity, and efficiency. For WoLaLa, this is a strong bridge source because it offers a principled middle path between purely formal grammatical description and purely usage-based explanation.

## Key Points
- The paper argues that information theory can provide a common mathematical framework for both formal and functional linguistics.
- It treats linguistic structure as the solution to constrained optimization problems rather than as either an arbitrary code or a purely autonomous formal object.
- The framework is meant to stay relatively neutral with respect to particular descriptive formalisms.
- The paper connects communication, complexity, and linguistic universals through efficiency-based explanation.
- For WoLaLa, it matters because it shows one way theory-facing linguistic explanation can remain compatible with data-driven modeling without collapsing into pure engineering success.

## Details
The article is explicit that formal and functional theories often seem to answer different questions: formal theories ask what a language is, while functional theories ask what language is for. Futrell and Hahn argue that information theory can bridge that divide. Their proposal treats communication as coding under constraints and linguistic complexity as something that can be characterized independently of any one descriptive formalism. On that basis, they argue that some linguistic regularities can be recovered as solutions to optimization problems, and they illustrate the framework through work on word order and related efficiency pressures.

That move is important for WoLaLa because it offers a more disciplined bridge than generic appeals to `usage` or `emergence`. It does not deny structure, and it does not reduce linguistic theory to benchmark performance. Instead, it asks whether language form can be explained as the outcome of pressures on communication and complexity. This makes the paper useful both for theory-facing debates about formalism and for cognition-facing debates about what kind of explanation language models might support. If LLMs are said to learn useful linguistic generalizations from large-scale data, sources like this help specify what sort of explanatory story could connect function, efficiency, and form without requiring the abandonment of rigorous structure.

## Interpretation
This source is best read as a bridge proposal rather than as a settled resolution of formal-versus-functional debate. Its significance lies in showing that one can demand mathematically explicit explanations of language function without discarding formal description, and that one can talk about optimization, complexity, and universals without simply equating engineering success with linguistic theory.
