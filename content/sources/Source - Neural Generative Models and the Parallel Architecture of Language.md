## Summary
Giulia Rambelli, Emmanuele Chersoni, Davide Testa, Philippe Blache, and Alessandro Lenci ask whether recent neural language models support parallel-architecture views of language in which syntax, semantics, and pragmatic enrichment interact without collapsing into a single strictly Fregean compositional stream. The paper is important for WoLaLa because it is one of the few currently indexed sources that explicitly tries to relate LLM behavior to a concrete linguistic architecture rather than to generic capability claims alone.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Neuroscientific Perspectives Overview|Neuroscientific Perspectives]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models Overview|Theoretical Linguistics and Language Models]]. It matters because it asks a sharper question than "are LLMs good at language?" The paper instead asks whether model behavior supports a specific architectural view on how syntax, semantics, and pragmatic enrichment interact. That makes it a useful bridge between language-theoretic interpretation and more cognition-facing comparison.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The paper is a critical review and outlook rather than a decisive experimental resolution. The open question is whether its comparisons provide enough evidence to treat LLMs as useful computational analogues of parallel architecture, or only as suggestive partial probes.
