## Summary
Dentella, Gunther, Murphy, Marcus, and Leivada test several current systems on language-comprehension tasks meant to probe underlying meaning rather than only surface continuation. They argue that the tested models often perform at or near chance and show unstable answer patterns, which they take to indicate weak sensitivity to the semantic structures humans rely on. For WoLaLa, the paper is a useful skeptical bridge because it ties understanding criticism to a specific evaluation design.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source matters because it directly targets a familiar overclaim: that broad task success can be read as evidence of human-like compositional understanding. Its benchmark is designed to pressure models where surface familiarity is not enough.

## Key Points
- The study evaluates comprehension-sensitive tasks rather than only generic next-step prediction.
- Human baselines make clear that the target ability is not exhausted by fluent continuation.
- The authors argue that the tested systems remain weak on underlying semantic integration.
- The paper supports a narrower interpretation of current LLM competence.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The benchmark is intentionally adversarial, and the debate over what it shows remains live. A central question is whether future models can improve on the task for principled reasons rather than benchmark adaptation alone.
