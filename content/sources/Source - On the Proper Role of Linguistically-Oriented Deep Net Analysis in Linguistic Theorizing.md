## Summary
Marco Baroni's chapter asks what linguistically oriented deep-net analysis should contribute to theoretical linguistics. Rather than treating probing work as a blank-slate test of innateness or as a mere engineering curiosity, Baroni argues that deep networks should be treated as explicit algorithmic theories that make predictions about utterance acceptability. For WoLaLa, the source matters because it clarifies how empirical deep-net results might inform linguistic theory without collapsing theory into benchmark performance.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source is valuable as a methodological bridge. It argues that modern deep nets matter for linguistics not because they automatically refute old positions, but because they can function as explicit, testable models whose theoretical role has to be stated more carefully.

## Key Points
- Baroni argues that linguistically oriented deep-net analysis has had little impact on linguistics partly because its theoretical significance is often left unclear.
- He rejects the idea that neural networks should be treated simply as blank slates that test nativist claims.
- Instead, he proposes treating deep nets as theories that make predictions about acceptability and grammatical behavior.
- The chapter frames deep-net analysis as complementary to mainstream algebraic approaches rather than as their automatic replacement.
- Its main contribution to WoLaLa is to give a disciplined answer to the question of how probing and targeted evaluation should feed into theoretical interpretation.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The chapter is more programmatic than evidential. It clarifies the role deep-net analysis could play in theorizing, but it does not by itself resolve the deeper disputes over what kind of explanation linguistics should demand from neural models.
