## Summary
Chang et al. survey LLM evaluation across three broad questions: what to evaluate, where to evaluate, and how to evaluate. The source matters for WoLaLa because it treats evaluation as a discipline rather than a loose collection of benchmarks, and because it spans both task performance and broader social-risk dimensions.

## WoLaLa Relevance
This source primarily supports [[../overviews/Applications and Best Practices Overview|Applications and Best Practices]] and secondarily [[../overviews/Linguistic Competence and Limitations Overview|Linguistic Competence and Limitations]]. Its main value is organizational. It groups LLM evaluation by task families, benchmark settings, and methodological choices, making it easier to see that evaluation is not only about downstream accuracy but also about benchmark choice, prompting regime, domain, and risk framing. For WoLaLa, it is a useful strand-6 map because it includes language tasks, reasoning, discipline-specific applications, and societal concerns in one evaluative picture.

## Limitation Or Open Question
The survey is broad rather than sharply selective. It is better as a field map than as a source of strong argument about which evaluation methods are most trustworthy. An open question is whether its breadth comes at the cost of treating linguistically diagnostic evaluation too much like one benchmark family among many.
